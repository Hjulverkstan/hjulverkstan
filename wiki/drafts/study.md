# The study

*A team space — very open. Anyone adds a question, a finding, a section, or a file of their own; nothing here is settled, and nothing needs to be finished before it is shared. Findings graduate into [`knowledge-structure.md`](knowledge-structure.md) — or into the principles themselves — when we agree they have earned it. Seeded 2026-08-21 by an agent from the principles; every area and question below is a proposal, not a syllabus.*

## Questions we stand with

The obvious questions the principles leave us holding:

- **Bounded heads.** What does learning science say about how much a head holds at once — and does it rhyme with what we know of context windows?
- **Nesting.** How deep can knowledge nest before navigation costs more than it saves? What do the structures we admire actually do?
- **Order.** Is a reading order one line or a tree? Do audiences share a common core and branch by outcome — and where does each branch stop?
- **Outcomes and audiences.** What is the full breadth of outcomes we want from this structure, and what roles come with each? (The inward study's first question.)
- **The boundary against code.** What must be written because the code cannot say it of itself — and nothing more?
- **Ground and status.** What does academic referencing translate to at our scale? How do others mark settledness without drowning in ceremony?
- **Compounding.** What sources ground the claim that unseen mistakes compound as cost, and that understanding early is cheaper than remedy late?
- **The vehicle.** Is markdown-in-a-repo the end form or the near-term one? What would a form look like that the client can read and the portal can serve?

## Areas to taste

Outward — each a fold someone can take, read around, and report back on:

- **Spec-driven development** — named on record, still unresearched; what does the practice actually hold?
- **Docs-as-code and documentation systems** — Diátaxis (tutorial / how-to / reference / explanation) splits docs by reader outcome; the big open handbooks (GitLab's, for one) run a company on one structure.
- **Encyclopedic practice** — Wikipedia's citation, neutrality and summary-style rules: grounded claims and marked status, at scale, by strangers.
- **Zettelkasten and linked notes** — knowledge as a field of relations with no top; what the entry-point compromise costs and buys.
- **Storytelling and pedagogy** — what makes a story engaging and comprehensible; scaffolding and curriculum design; how a good lesson orders knowledge for a bounded head.
- **Literate programming** — Knuth's bet that a program is literature for humans first; the oldest form of "the code is not the important artifact".
- **Tacit and explicit knowledge** — the knowledge-management lineage; what of ours should stay personal or verbal, on purpose.
- **How agents read** — context engineering, harnesses, what changes about an entry when the reader is a model.
- **Onboarding in the wild** — how open-source projects and organisations get a stranger productive; what our client's own onboarding (mechanics, the portal) can borrow from it.
