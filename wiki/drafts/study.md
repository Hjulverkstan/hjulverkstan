## Where the study stands

Three things now sit beside this file, and they are meant to be read in this order.

[`chart.md`](chart.md) is the outward half's first pass — eleven territories tasted in parallel, each with what the practice holds, what it says to the ladder, what to steal, what it costs, and its sources. Its ground was gathered in one pass and **no human has followed the links**, so every citation is a lead until somebody reads it; two claims are flagged as things not to cite. Its eight proposals to the ladder are the thing to argue with.

[`code.md`](code.md) is the sharpest input we have, and it did not come from here. It was written in **OpenLight**, Jona's own project, whose spec tree is chasing the same question from the other side; our ladder and our chart were inputs to it, and it names three of the chart's corrections as folded in. It compresses the fifteen rungs into **two values, eight facts and six principles**, each principle carrying the trace of which value met which fact — so a principle can be disputed by denying the value or denying the fact, and there is no third way in. It then does what our draft does not yet: a method with ordered steps, a named test suite with a runner and a moment for each test, and an ordered list of what is still open. It is proposed, not law, in its own project as much as in ours. **It is for the team to work over**, and whether it supersedes the ladder is our ruling to make, not an agent's and not a sister project's.

The inward half has not started.

## Questions we stand with

The obvious questions the principles leave us holding. Where the chart moved one it says so; where the code answers one it says so — and an answered question is only answered as a proposal.

- **Bounded heads.** How much does a head hold at once, and does it rhyme with a context window? *Charted: roughly four chunks, not seven; chunking is stored structure, not capacity. The code takes the chart's correction and drops the equation — bounded differently, and what the two share is the cost of finding things again.*
- **Nesting.** How deep before navigation costs more than it saves? *Charted: two levels, three at most, breadth over depth, labels judged by whether a reader picks the right child from the label alone. The code keeps those as leads, not law, and demotes hierarchy to a filing aid over the field rather than the structure itself.*
- **Order.** Is a reading order one line or a tree? *Answered in the code, as a proposal: neither. A **reading** is a published path for one goal, the entry publishes several, and structure branches by outcome — never by demographic, since the audience of a reading is whoever wants what it delivers.*
- **Outcomes and audiences.** What breadth of outcomes do we want, and what roles come with each? *Still the inward study's first question. The code makes it a precondition: map the outcomes actually wanted before publishing the readings.*
- **The boundary against code.** What must be written because the code cannot say it of itself? *Charted and then ruled into a line: prose owns why and forces, code owns what and how, and prose restating what the code says is a defect, not thoroughness.*
- **Ground and status.** What does referencing translate to at our scale, without ceremony? *Charted: closed vocabularies, dated, and owned. The code makes status part of the writing — grounded, referenced, or marked open, no third option — and adds the rule the chart implied: a claim inherits the doubt of the weakest thing it stands on unless it says why not.*
- **Compounding.** What grounds the claim? *Charted, with a correction the code carries: absorptive capacity and knowledge as a non-rival good are the ground; the 1:10:100 ratio and the "IBM Systems Sciences Institute" study behind it are folklore and must not be cited.*
- **The vehicle.** Is markdown-in-a-repo the end form? *Untouched here. In OpenLight it is answered by building elsewhere — the code is written to be prior to any vehicle, with markdown as the study form.*

## Areas to taste

The nine seeded areas have each had one pass in [`chart.md`](chart.md). A second, differently-briefed pass is the honest check and has not been run — the fleet was briefed by us and carried our framing into its own findings. What the chart did not reach:

- **Design systems and Figma** as a linkable source of design truth — the open thread the board already carries.
- **Localisation** — what a Swedish-reading client and Swedish-speaking mechanics do to an English wiki; whether the structure or only the leaves translate.
- **Derived documentation** — OpenAPI, Javadoc, JSDoc, generated tables: where "generate, don't retype" gets tested.
- **Teaching in a non-developer register** — task cards, demonstration, how the mechanics actually learn the portal and the trade.
- **Who pays for documentation** in a pro-bono project staffed by interns and consultants between assignments.

## What the code leaves us to decide

Its own *Open* list is ordered by bite and is the best agenda for the team hour. The ones that are ours specifically, rather than OpenLight's:

- **Whether the code replaces the ladder, sits beside it, or gets taken in parts.** It is written to supersede; nothing obliges us to take it whole.
- **What it knowingly does not fold in:** the ladder's rung on personal knowledge and its speaker's ownership, and everything client-side. Both matter here more than they do there — the transcripts practice is built on the first, and the second is the delivery goal.
- **The human reader is not blank.** Onboarding research says mentors and response latency dominate, not documentation. The code names this as open; for us it is concrete, because our next readers are interns and a client.
- **Whether the two projects' registers should agree** — theirs is spec / research / sketch / log; ours is drafts / log / transcripts and a spec that is still an open word.
