# Holon & brief
A brief is one piece of text/prose that gives understanding.
In practice, it is a heading with prose. Its face is the heading and the first paragraph, the face says what it gives you by continuing reading.

A holon is a brief together with everything beneath it, a whole that is also a part.
A holon can be read alone, it belongs to the understanding above it, and is broken down beneath.

Every brief is a top of a holon.

## How files are joined:
1. A folder with its entry file. The entry is the brief; the files beside it are its parts.
2. A file's headings. A heading with its prose is a brief; its subheadings are its parts.
3. A paragraph with bullets. The paragraph is the brief and the bullets its parts. Bullets go no deeper and can't be linked to.
4. A link standing alone in its own paragraph places another file there as a part, shown as a card (practice §3). That is how files join into one body. A link inside a sentence places nothing; it's only an offer to the reader.

# How to build a holon?
A holon is built in steps, an agent does (most of) the writing and people decide at a few points: what is worth knowing, what is true.

1. People are needed. A model infers, it does not comprehend. Importance, new knowledge comes from comprehension.
2. A holons step
   - Choosing what to build: biggest wins from approved sources
   - Agree on an outline before prose: who has to agree and what does agreed mean? Does everyone need to read through everytime, or is someone responsible and trusted to make decision?
   - Write and fresh heads: agents write and read
   - Check against the real world, confidence markings, lost context etc.
   - Read as the reader: who is it for and does that translate?
   - Ratifying
   - (Acknowledge new findings and procedures(look back on logs/history?), feed it to the rule if appropriate)
3. Keep peoples involvement small and decisive, don't write faster than people read.
4. What's open.
