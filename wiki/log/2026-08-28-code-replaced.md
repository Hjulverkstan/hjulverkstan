# 2026-08-28 — The code replaced whole; the first peer review

Two things happened to the same file. Recorded together because they converged.

**Replaced.** [`drafts/code.md`](../drafts/code.md) — the version brought in on the 25th ([entry](2026-08-25-code-brought-in.md)) is superseded whole by OpenLight's current draft, which is roughly a fifth of its length. That entry stands as written; records are not rewritten.

What the new draft states is only a core. **One limit:** nobody holds everything, and a person forgetting and a context window filling are the same wall in two currencies — better tools widen what is held and cheapen the reaching, but do not change how understanding works. **Two primitives:** the *fold*, a piece of writing that stands on its own, now earned through the paper image — work runs linear until it must leave its thread, and what the thread needed back is the conclusion, so the crossed stretch is folded and the detail pleated inside for whoever opens it; and the *link*, the one join, always an offer and never a missing piece. **Two laws**, one for each side of compounding: *nothing arrives before its ground* — meaning before use, purpose before machinery, every stopping point leaving the reader whole, and a declared debt where the subject genuinely forces the other order; and *the ground stays true* — one home per fact, confidence carried in the writing, with settled meaning settled for now given what it stands on.

**What went, and why it matters.** The registers, the readings, the frontier, the ten-step method and the named test suite are all gone from the document. The draft now says plainly that nothing has been built under the code yet, and that how folds compose into a whole is to be *observed, not prescribed*. Its closing link points at a first proof that does not exist. Narrowing to what is actually held is the code obeying its own second law.

**First peer review.** Eric read the earlier draft and the README and left the first outside review of this work. His notes and the rewrite converged without either seeing the other: he asked for *fold* to be explained rather than assumed, and it now is; he named *chunk*, *field* and *substrate* as words used before they were earned, and all three are gone; he asked that the text around a link say what is on the other side before you click, and the code now states that as law. Two asks the code did **not** answer, both open and both his to press: visuals and diagrams as a carrier for human readers, and a plainer restatement at the densest points. The lesson worth keeping is that the review's value was not in catching errors but in showing where the writing had run ahead of its reader — law 1, found from outside by someone who had not read the law.

**Rewritten.** [`drafts/study.md`](../drafts/study.md) whole — the code's core restated accurately, the review recorded, the questions re-marked where the new draft reopens what the old one had answered (order and depth are open again, on purpose). Board lines in [`drafts/knowledge-structure.md`](../drafts/knowledge-structure.md) updated, and one added for the first proof.
