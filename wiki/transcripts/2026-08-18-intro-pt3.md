## How to process the transcripts · 09:10

Jona's gut feeling: give the transcripts to Fable, the strongest model, and ask it first to write them as good prose, because there is a strong silver lining in a transcript anyway — the folds are there, and after a while Jona even named them as he spoke: "I am folding, I am folding up".

### Outline first, and how Claude edits prose · 09:52

When Claude models edit prose — Jona's proficient experience is with these — they never rewrite everything, even when the text is outdated enough that a full rewrite would be best: they move paragraph by paragraph and edit. That doesn't work, and one sentence about it is enough for the model to understand why: prose has a disposition, a meaning, a silver lining; like the folds, there are seams and a flow; sections and subsections carry it; and where a concept cannot be understood without a prerequisite concept, that is a dependency.

So with a large amount of prose Jona asks it to outline first — to talk about and understand the meaning and the dependencies — and then to verify, since there is always room for mistakes; verifying an outline is far easier and more effective than verifying prose, and it might run three sub-agents at it from different angles. It can test whether this is the most organic way to speak about the subject: simulate the prose without having it, and remedy the mistakes before they are written. Jona's own case is a day's talking in a session, at maybe 50% of context, to be boiled down into the document of truth — all findings, properly, and how they override earlier ideas. Transcripts are kind of the same thing.

### Hierarchy versus the folds of dialogue · 12:24

The uncertain part: the normal structure is markdown files and directories, which are hierarchical, and dialogue is not necessarily — yesterday folded maybe five deep. Nobody knows. A knowledge structure essentially — the technology Jona works on — has no top and no hierarchy, just a field of relations; but we are doing it the normal way, which means dealing with the fact of an entry point, and that is good practice, since every new person and every agent needs one. Hierarchy is not necessarily a problem, just difficult.
