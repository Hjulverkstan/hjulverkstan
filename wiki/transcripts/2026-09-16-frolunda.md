## 1. The pilot: why Alten records this · pt1 14:56

The pilot is a body of knowledge about how to build knowledge and how to work with it. Elsewhere Alten builds "AI accelerators", engineering a specific technology that makes a difference. The pilot builds no technology: it is the meaning of things, an ontology, and it makes AI better at the work. It is in its early stages, and it is getting a different kind of traction in Alten than before.

### 1.1 The thesis · pt1 19:56

Value is commonly generated through comprehension: not by humans as such, but on a capitalistic planet, organisations make money from a comprehension edge. That edge may be skill, like a team of great surgeons, or a product built on it, or the product may be the edge itself.

Comprehension is carried by individuals, who find their roles by holding it, and joining it toward a common goal is hard. Alten's common goal is to build great products cheaply, so business people and engineers must understand each other, and that is full of friction and suffering, as in any organisation.

Two forces act on knowledge. Reading is linear: start at the top of a wiki and the prose must not run ahead of you, telling you what you cannot yet understand. But starting from the details delays the comprehension and buries you in what you may not care about. So the other force: the biggest wins first, the most important comprehension as fast as possible, broken down into small, easy steps, never force-fed. The team has called it a win-based knowledge structure.

The structure is never weighed down by addition, since every detail has a place, down to how to change a washer without destroying the thread. Everyone reads the same top, the common goal, and diverges further down by what they need, so it only compounds.

It is less a way of structuring knowledge than a way of approaching progress. Lobotomise ourselves and start on Hjulverkstan tomorrow knowing nothing: we would not run into details, we would take small grounded steps toward the comprehension most needed. So it is a rule for how we collaborate and build, with a core in place and now spreading into how digital applications are built. Hjulverkstan grows at the pace it does for the same reason: onboarding is sensitive, and it grows organically.

### 1.2 What AI needs is grounding · pt1 15:46

To do Hjulverkstan from scratch, a person needs Save the Children's background and how it operates, the vision and the soft values, how the workshops work and govern themselves, and the technology and how it is worked with. When those cornerstones are filled, an intern or a junior can be taken in and become grounded and whole, without gaps that live in someone's head or must be studied hard.

AI needs the same. People take a model into a case and it goes this way or that, which is why some call each new model great and others stupid. Ground it in the knowledge needed to make a difference, and it is a different AI.

### 1.3 The practice already exists for developers · pt1 17:18

Giving this kind of AI to non-developers would probably need a whole foundation of technology. Developers already have one. Harnesses, applications from the AI providers such as Claude Code or Codex, run a session connected to a folder on the computer, and every file in it can be read and edited. With version control every change is recorded, so nothing is lost. The knowledge is kept as markdown, text files as simple as a TXT file, and developers have long written what they will build into such files before building it.

A conversation with ChatGPT on the web gathers comprehension and evolves it, but leaves it nowhere to go. The harness puts it somewhere, so the novel part is only the ontology.

### 1.4 AI lives in the structure · pt1 27:23

A session starts in the knowledge structure, and what is talked about lands in it. Before coding you talk about what to build, by the rule: the most important first, in small whole steps. Jona spent five hours building a front end for navigating the structure the Friday before, and has never experienced AI like it, because it started grounded in the code and the background. With the knowledge in place, he thinks Hjulverkstan could perhaps be regenerated in a few days.

That is the debt: Hjulverkstan never had documentation. It has moved slowly, as a side project of Jona's, with everything held by him.

### 1.5 The ambition: one medium with the client · pt1 28:57

The ambition is to share the structure and its interface with Save the Children, so that onboarding at the workshops runs through the same system, and to break the silo between client and Alten. Reading something about how the workshop works that is wrong, you comment that you disagree; reading something right, you stamp it.

Mauricio grounded it in two cases. Someone who wants to improve the application goes into the repository, understands in a short time what is needed, and contributes. And, not yet but possibly, a new mechanic is onboarded quickly from knowledge structured to be easy to take in.

The vision, simply named, is a medium that interconnects us so that comprehension is carried together as easily as possible, and onboarding and offboarding are no longer things. It has been Jona's dream since he was a child: computers today, social media included, touch on the capability but do not enable such a medium. Why spend time onboarding someone when this can be done instead?

### 1.6 Filling it · pt1 41:32

Now that the pilot works, it is time to fill it with Hjulverkstan's knowledge. Few meetings are needed, since what is said is now saved and used: AI embodying the rule restructures the recordings. By the time the structure is filled and grounded, Save the Children can take part in it, without promises.

The core is one open-source module, currently on Jona's account, and Hjulverkstan is an extension of it. Alten has one closed-source client in the pilot, another most likely joining in a few weeks, and Jona's own project. What the knowledge learns from the closed clients, short of trade secrets, comes back to the core, since the learnings are not about the details [?]. So it compounds both ways, and in a few weeks several clients, Hjulverkstan included, go through the same kind of sessions and compound from their different angles. Hjulverkstan is the easy one, being open source, without the closed clients' authentication and security concerns.

### 1.7 How Jona came to it · pt1 10:56

Since Jona and Mauricio took an AI plunge in the winter, Jona has felt he cannot work the usual way any more, neither in the normal business model nor in his traditional role as a developer. Building things gives a technical intuition: some ways of building compound, others limit. Organic growth is not running ahead of yourself but taking realistic steps, and those are the steps that compound.

His side project is technically heavy, a reconstruction of operating systems, computers and how we interact with them, and building it with AI taught him what kind of force AI is. By the summer he had gone deep, bottom up, into details he knew hold potential, without coming from the top, from what the goals are, which he calls the more realistic approach. He surfaced just as this group came together.

The first week's sessions were recorded and transcribed, and the ontology uncovered in them, what knowledge is, what AI is, what we do when we build, already made AI perform better. A week later a meeting with Chiaco and Samuel widened it into a vision of knowledge sharing and organic growth. The pilot is now used with paying customers too, and moves fast. For Jona it is revolutionary, about rights and about human nature.

## 2. Hjulverkstan as Save the Children sees it · pt1 29:57

Hjulverkstan is a holistic take on life, on bikes and on the people who work there, and that is what Save the Children nationally struggles to see. Daniel Andersson laid it out in answer to the pilot. It bears on everything built for Hjulverkstan, because the goals and the measures of effect must carry it.

### 2.1 Not a box · pt1 29:57

When Hjulverkstan is explained nationally, others want to do it too without knowing what it is or why, and they box it in: preventive work against youth at risk. It is not; it is bikes, culture and community. The goals and the measures of effect must hold that: is what we do relevant, and when is it time to close a Hjulverkstan?

Everything is for everyone who wants to take part, so the answer is never no. The question is how to build things resilient enough to hold such a place. Daniel Andersson has asked the effect-measuring team how to measure the Frölunda workshop's effect on the community: what is its core, Daniel and his personality and approach to people, or something else?

### 2.2 Each place is different · pt1 36:50

The organisation wants one goal for Save the Children, which is like measuring a cloud. In Frölunda it may be a really good bike repair shop, in Biskopsgården a meeting place, in Backa it is about the gangs. Nothing in a community can be copied and pasted, yet the frame says poor people, poor area. Conceptualising Hjulverkstan is impossible, and the dialogue about measuring it has been flat.

Mauricio recognised it: models of governance make you fit a framework in order to progress, and complexity like this is hard to measure. Jona agreed: governance runs through frameworks, what Hjulverkstan does is unbounded and open, and Alten's pilot does not fit its own frameworks either.

Jona has a friend who works on the same from governance, on measurables, and from his NGO experience measuring is how funding works. He sees the knowledge structure as a foundation for it.

### 2.3 Anchored locally · pt1 33:04

The work is anchored in the local area, with the people who live there. If mothers come by wanting a group to talk about the important issues, a group of mothers starts. Relevance must be measured too.

Asked why it is organic: responsibility and trust, and the idea that we are in this together. Save the Children could make its own money on its own, but that is not how it works.

### 2.4 Rights and dreams · pt1 35:12

Save the Children is a rights organisation, so it is a thorn locally when schools, the city or anyone harms children. It also needs a pull, so that what happens is seen as important: "we want them to hate that they love us", not love to hate us.

The work has become mechanical, jobs, school and grades, when children have a right to dreams. At a dream camp, sixth graders talked about what they need in order to dream. In these areas children no longer dream, because needs come first, as in Maslow.

### 2.5 Complexity · pt1 33:36

What the organisation does not understand, in Daniel Andersson's view, is that the work in Gothenburg is about handling complexity. A problem seen in an individual is taken to the group and to the structural level, poverty for example. Structural solutions do not reach the individual, and working only with individuals changes nothing for the group or the structure.

### 2.6 Collective impact and local mandate · pt1 38:55

Save the Children has models for collective impact: civil society, the municipality, business and academia set a baseline for an area together, agree common goals and build a chain of value toward them. Some units still work that way, fewer now that it is about finding money and setting up work. The idea to return to is the responsibility to contribute and to give others a voice, which is what rights are.

That responsibility includes not shutting down other actors doing good but complementing them, Daniel's own side business for instance, and cooperation needs measuring too.

The staff are in charge of where their workshop goes: ideas from local needs are how it progresses, with freedom, mandate and responsibility. Onboarding into that way of thinking is hard.

### 2.7 The new place · pt1 05:57

Save the Children has about 80 people in Gothenburg's disadvantaged areas, and Hjulverkstan is in five of them. Daniel Andersson is leaving his role to start a new place with the city of Gothenburg, Returhuset, taking over a place that has existed for 30 years and closes this winter. It is a place to train and work and be evaluated, for youth at risk like the young adults at Hjulverkstan, and for others who are not at risk. In about three years it will have some 60 places: a café, lunch, a garden, a tool shop for bikes and textiles and the digital side, with course modules such as welding, programming and AI. It needs a baseline of where Gothenburg stands on youth at risk, and not only on risk.

Partners are joining: a community called School [?] doing creative programming, art, music, video, dance and psychoanalysis; a group in Gothenburg building gaming platforms and games played between Gothenburg, Tokyo and Montreal; and Reach for Change, with social innovation labs from next year, funded also by Arvsfonden [?]. It is about visions and dreams and living life fully, not only school or work. Jona shares the passion, and has always felt much between the lines of this project, able to grow organically.

## 3. The workshop in Frölunda · pt1 43:10

The workshop repairs bikes for people nearby, lends bikes and tools, and is a place for young adults to work and learn. It runs on paper, a folder, a spreadsheet and private phones, and wants one system instead. Practicalities had been discussed with the workshop before, but never put into a structure. Samuel had said the workshop's staff need onboarding, to the application and to the work it is one piece of, so Jona wanted the whole shape before drilling down: every detail is valuable once it has its place. Daniel, the instructor, took the room through it.

### 3.1 The place and the people · pt2 01:46

The expertise is bikes, though people ask about other things too. Beside the workshop runs Fixoteket, lending tools, mostly power tools and sewing machines, so that not everyone has to buy a drill. It is a good project, found in several places in Gothenburg, but here it takes little time and gets little. The workshop is open three days a week, three hours at a time, opening at three. It restores bikes to lend, and five to seven are out now.

Daniel has been instructor for a year, part-time, after a year in Biskopsgården, and is responsible for the young adults who work there, three at the moment. For them it is part of life but not the end station: they stay about a year on average, some longer, depending on where they are in life. Daniel sees his own part as making the system work in the workshop.

He has laid out three stations with three bike stands, the tools taped in green or yellow per station. Each station is someone's responsibility: nobody takes tools from it, and its keeper keeps it clean, since Daniel is tired of cleaning. He takes a station himself, and is equipping them all for everyday work.

Today customers are on paper notes taped to their bikes, borrow bikes in a folder, returns in a spreadsheet, and customers are texted from staff's private phones, since there is no workshop phone. It keeps things afloat but is not sustainable. The workshop tried the web app before, and Daniel tried its functions and wrote feedback in GitHub, but it never launched from the workshop's side, partly its own fault; his supervisor Jafet [?] quit. Now the season allows adopting it fully, and the workshop is "quite desperate" for a system that works for all the workshops.

### 3.2 What the workshop needs from a system · pt2 07:40

Mauricio's aim was the business value and intent the system has for the workshop. The biggest pain is that nothing is integrated: the workshop wants one system for all the workshops and all their functions, every bike in one place, borrow bikes filterable by when they are available, an overview of the work, the resources and when bikes come back, and help to plan.

Daniel thinks it would work for every workshop. Each brings something different to its community, but the practical work is the same: all take customers' bikes, repair them and give them back, and all are meant to lend bikes. The protocol on the wall is the same everywhere. Jona added that Samuel said the same: the workshops share a way of working, and the portal is a tool to fit it.

It is successful when it is part of the way of working, streamlined, and replaces the notes: a customer comes, you press a button, ask for the information, and it is stored where it is easy to overview. Everyone is meant to use it, the young adults included: whoever handles a bike updates it.

It must also be fast. At the spring meeting Daniel looked sceptical because the portal asked too much of each customer. Many jobs are quick fixes of twenty minutes and should not be bureaucratic, so there must be a spectrum. Since then only what is absolutely required is required, after feedback taken up by Mauricio [?].

The other edge of the sword: when colleagues skip the phone number, the bike has to be kept until the customer comes asking. So the right level of information, every time.

It runs on one computer, since everyone already comes to one place to write on paper. Jona wants one too, because the information is sensitive: the computer should lock quickly and have two accounts, one with more permissions and its password saved in its own browser profile, and the operators' with fewer.

### 3.3 Repair: taking a bike in · pt2 33:52

Repair is drop-in; nobody books. In high season bikes are sometimes sent home, so Daniel is open to booking, as the professional shops, Sportson [?] for one, prefer.

When a customer comes in (Daniel shouts "come in", he cannot run to the door), they assess the bike together: what works, what should change, is it realistic. The estimate is a wait of two or three weeks, never a promise. The note takes the name, the phone number and the symptoms, and is taped to the bike.

The phone number is always taken; details about the bike mostly are not, no model, no brand, no photo. A flat front tyre can be fixed for collection at six the same day, with no service or maintenance.

Some parts, easy to change, cannot be given for free: Daniel refers the customer to an affordable shop, sometimes showing online exactly which part to buy, and mounts it when they come back with it. Cables, brake pads and the like are given away, or repairs would never happen.

### 3.4 Repair: the work and the handover · pt2 15:37

In low season a bike can be worked on straight away; usually they queue, so the date a bike came in should be stored automatically, to see which is next. Whoever finishes a bike puts it out front for collection, takes the next, reads its note, hangs it up and works on it.

Customers usually blame the chain, and the chain is usually fine: without knowing bikes it is hard to place a noise. So the workshop makes its own assessment.

If a service was agreed, the protocol is followed: wash, tyres, brakes, chain, gears and more [?], a guide for the young adults and for Daniel alike. If it is urgent or the workshop is busy, it is only the flat tyre. Daniel likes that not every bike gets the protocol, since some people only want a lamp adjusted or a saddle raised; Daniel Andersson and Chiaco perhaps want it more streamlined, and there will always be exceptions.

Daniel shows the young adults how to do the work, and those who have been there long know it. It ends with a test ride.

When a bike is put out front the customer is texted at once, so it is not forgotten: done, with the opening hours, but not what was done. At collection customers often have questions, and sometimes get a small lecture, on adjusting rear gears for instance. Whoever hands the bike over says what was done, preferably the one who did the work, but not always, which is where the record in the system helps.

A customer's request to find a part is an exception, not part of the service: at a mobile event day in Frölunda, a customer with a failed rear wheel was unsure whether to invest, and Daniel took his number to call if he found one.

### 3.5 Lending bikes and tools · pt2 22:39

Lending bikes and lending tools run the same way, on the same sheet and the same contract. For a tool, posters in the window say what can be borrowed. A customer asks for a drill, Daniel checks the shelf, and if it is out the sheet says who has it and when it is due. A new borrower signs a paper agreeing to what borrowing means, including responsibility if something is lost; there have never been problems. Borrowers are numbered, "loan taker 19", in a Google Sheet, and Daniel is unsure this meets GDPR. It could be much better digital.

Nobody required the contract for bikes. It existed for tools, and signing makes people feel responsible, so Daniel carried it over; some workshops have none. Jona thinks it did not exist when the portal was built.

A bank ID signature was discussed before and ruled out, since it cannot be demanded of everyone who comes. A phone is required, a parent's for a child, and is the standard contact. Since the portal already has customers' phone numbers, SMS notifications and GDPR deletion of a person's details, Jona sees a simple digital contract: an SMS with a link, read, answer yes. It first goes to whoever runs the workshops (Chiaco, Samuel or the new person) and to Save the Children's legal support; a pin for when it is time. Daniel thinks digital could work as well as paper for the sense of responsibility.

### 3.6 Restoring bikes to lend · pt2 28:36

Bikes to lend come from landlords clearing their bike rooms. Daniel arranges it by mail, rents a large car, texts colleagues whether anyone wants extra hours, and brings the bikes to the storage room. About half can be restored; the rest become spare parts or go to the scrapyard. When customers' bikes allow, one is restored to lendable condition. It is good learning for the young adults: many parts to change, no rush, done thoroughly.

### 3.7 Scope and safety · pt2 30:12

The workshop offers basic service, and sends people to a professional workshop for more: it lacks the tools, and the gap in the young adults' knowledge is too large. Routines are being written for what service to give, different for children's and adults' bikes. At the end the instructor checks the brakes and what else was done.

Safety is a big thing, and Daniel wants specific education in it, perhaps at Returhuset. A workshop day there, to which Daniel is invited, will have stations on what the young adults practise and on diagnosing a bike.

The young adults may be educated more structurally, toward a diploma, and there brands matter: someone may fix Shimano gears but not another make [?]. That plan had not been told to Alten before. The new place will focus more on education, lecture-like and toward diplomas, so brand data is more relevant there than in the workshop, where young adults ask when they need help.

### 3.8 Giving and receiving bikes · pt2 31:43

Bikes are given and received now and then, but cautiously and without a routine: giving things away is sensitive, and there must not be a line of people asking for a bike. A mother came with her daughter's bike, too worn to be worth the hours; she was asked to come back the next week and swap it, so that it felt like giving something and getting something. Jona recalled the organised form from before: events where people learn to repair a bike and then take it home.

## 4. The portal against the workflows · pt2 05:02

The portal is simple and not yet optimal, but in Jona's words production ready and GDPR approved. What is said here is as Jona told it and showed it on the development environment.

### 4.1 Where it stands · pt2 05:02

The spring meeting, before the summer, with mechanics from Frölunda and other workshops, was the portal's first meeting with the real world after a long vacuum without collaboration. From its feedback Jessica and Andre, interns then and Alten employees now, fixed bugs and behaviour and added what production needed, GDPR features among them, so it went from untested to tested.

It is built "power user" style: every page is a table with filters, and what the table shows can be edited. The recipe repeats with no custom paths, which is brutal in places, and it still takes more clicks than it should.

A question is asked through the filters. The next repair is tickets in this workshop, of type repair, on customer-owned bikes, with status not started, sorted by date created. It is not one click, but it is possible, and filters persist. A dashboard is coming, where the usual questions, such as the next five in the queue, are pre-made.

### 4.2 Tickets and their statuses · pt2 43:04

The portal is tickets, of four types: giving away and receiving, both unusual, and repair and rental. A ticket's statuses are the points in the workflow where you must go to the computer, the gates in the process. Because the system records what was done and brings you to it at fixed points, it can carry what a handover by someone else would miss.

A repair starts as *ready* once it is received and filled in, goes *in progress* when work starts, and *complete* when it is done, which sends the SMS automatically; it is *closed* when the customer has it back. A rental is one step shorter: *ready*, *in progress* when the bike is taken, *closed* when it is returned. A rental's end date, even a loose one, raises a notification if the bike is not back, and its start date could be set in the future, though that has not been grounded in reality. Whether start and end are required Jona did not remember.

Creating a ticket today means jumping between pages: find or create the customer, find or create the bike, then make the ticket. A shortcut makes a ticket from a bike, but did not preselect the bike, a bug. A customer-owned bike without a ticket shows a red warning: the steps are isolated, so pausing between them lets the workflow fall apart.

Customers can be people or organisations; so far only people, though Daniel thinks organisations may come. Customer-owned bikes get an increasing number, the workshop's own a short letter code.

The ticket names the employee responsible, and another can be assigned. There are no profiles and no personal logins: one login is used on the workshop's computer, and logins (users) are separate from employees; users are controlled, and those without the password must use that computer [?].

The repair description and comments are free text, with no checkboxes, so a simple shared vocabulary of keywords makes repairs searchable.

Bikes the workshop owns and lends appear on the public website with an image and all details, so they must be filled in properly, and Daniel would gladly have more required for them. And they are borrowed, not rented: the portal should say borrow.

### 4.3 The wizard · pt2 52:03

A redesign from the spring, the wizard, known as "Trinity", is not implemented. It asks what you are doing, with the location preselected. For a repair: external or internal, then the description, then a person or an organisation, searched among existing customers with their details and previous tickets, to check it is the right one; then the bike, or a new one. It at least halves the clicks, and since it is applied only at the end, the workflow cannot break midway. The power-user pages remain for when they are needed.

Searching first is deliberate: asking the name and searching avoids duplicate customers. An intern asked whether customers would fill in a form. No: staff ask the same questions and enter them on the computer, which Daniel prefers to paper.

### 4.4 What the workshop wants next · pt2 57:23

A queue of what to repair next, as a dashboard.

A reminder when a repair has waited too long, not to tell customers but so a bike is not forgotten in a corner. Rentals have an end date and repairs do not, but a configured age could raise it on the dashboard.

A text on completion saying what was done, perhaps from checkboxes following the protocol.

Structured detail where a repair is complex, a derailleur say, so that what a mechanic has repaired builds toward a résumé or diploma.

The digital contract of [3.5](#35-lending-bikes-and-tools--pt2-2239).

Tool lending stays out: Fixoteket was deliberately left out of the portal by the partnership. At the new place, with a large inventory and a different, organic take on it, knowledge and inventory might come to live in the same substrate, beyond this product.

Daniel offered to be a step between the workshop and Alten, passing information and questions on so that not everything goes through Daniel Andersson; Jona wants the partnership to grow on that. Talk goes on WhatsApp; anything about the application goes in GitHub, so it is already in the system.
